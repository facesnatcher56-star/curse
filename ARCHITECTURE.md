# Architecture

> **Art direction (gritty and versatile) governs every asset: see [docs/ART_DIRECTION.md](docs/ART_DIRECTION.md) and [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md).**

A Godot 4 click-to-move ARPG: fixed 3/4 camera, weighty melee, wave arena. Scripts live in `game/`, tunable data in `data/`.

## Layout

| Path | What lives there |
| --- | --- |
| `game/main.gd` | Builds the world (arena, hero, camera, HUD, menus) and creates the `RunDirector`. ~4 KB. |
| `game/run/run_director.gd` | The run: wave composition and spawning, kill tracking, the reward offered between waves. |
| `game/player.gd` | The hero node: input, cursor picking, orchestration of the components below, hit hooks. |
| `game/player/` | `PlayerStats` (mana/stamina/potions, cooldowns, equipment + affix queries, regen), `PlayerMovement` (click-to-move, locomotion animation, dodge roll), `SkillController` (hotkeys, aim preview, starting/ticking skills, combo, strike effects). |
| `game/skills/` | `SkewerSkill`, `LeapSkill`, `EarthshatterSkill` (the ultimate: charges from damage dealt, `PlayerStats.ult_charge`): self-contained skill state machines. |
| `game/enemy.gd`, `game/enemies/` | `Enemy` is the shared body (stats from its `EnemyDef`, aggro/stun, movement helpers, attack slots). `EnemyBehavior` subclasses decide what it does: `MeleeBehavior`, `PouncerBehavior`, `SpitterBehavior`, `BloaterBehavior`, `SupportBehavior`. `AcidGlob` and `HazardZone` are their effects. |
| `game/actor.gd` | Base of hero and enemies: health, hit reaction, burn/frost/ward/daze, ragdoll, gib bursts. A heavy knock-back that ends against a wall, a prop or another monster (`Actor._knock_impact`) costs a little damage and a stun, and knocks the monster it hits down; `apply_snare` is the slow ranged monsters get when hit while backing away (`snare_on_hit` in their `EnemyDef` params). |
| `game/data/` | Resource classes (`EnemyDef`, `SkillDef`, `AffixDef`) and their loaders (`EnemyDb`, `SkillDb`, `AffixDb`). |
| `data/` | The `.tres` files those loaders read: `data/enemies`, `data/skills`, `data/affixes`. |
| `game/ground_look.gd` | The ground shader (`GroundLook.material`): four scales of noise in world space (broad stains, clods and cracks from a cellular noise, gravel, grit) with the relief worked out from the same heights, so the plaza's earth and the Crypt Road's ribbon (`edge_fade`, fades with vertex alpha) are detailed down to a few centimetres and join without a seam. Replaced the single stretched noise texture. |
| `tools/author_attack_anims.gd` | Authors the basic-attack clips on the Knight's own rig (`godot --headless --path . --script tools/author_attack_anims.gd`, rewrites the `atk_*` and `atk2_*` clips in `assets/models/knight2/anims.res`). `atk_*` are the Meshy swings with body motion layered on (hips and chest coil and drive, lean, knees take the weight, head stays on the target); `atk2_*` are for two-handers (`two_hand_clips` in a weapon's profile): both arms are solved so both hands stay on the grip. Basic swings have no blade ribbon any more (Power Strike keeps its own). Look at them with `-- --clipsheet=NAME --closeup [--equip=Greatsword]`. Test: `--only=twohand`. |
| `tools/blender/make_fists.py`, `CharacterModel._give_fists` | The Knight's hands: Meshy made them flat and open (no finger bones), so a grip went through the palm. `fist_r.glb` / `fist_l.glb` (closed gauntlets, built in the hand bone's frame around the sword's grip point) ride each hand through a mount node that follows the hand bone's un-shrunk pose; the open hand mesh is shrunk away by scaling the hand bones to ~0 after every animation frame (`_after_mixer`). Weapons attach to the mount too (`_mount_for`). `idle_rest` (authored in `tools/author_attack_anims.gd`: the sword lying over the right shoulder) plays after `PlayerMovement.REST_AFTER` s of standing about out of combat, and on the main menu. Look: `-- --handshot[=CLIP:T] [--body] [--equip=Greatsword]`. Test: `--only=fists`. |
| `game/skills/weapon_throw_skill.gd`, `thrown_weapon.gd`, `throw_preview.gd` | Weapon Throw (an assignable skill, `SkillDb` id `throw`, no hotkey of its own): hold to wind up (`wthrow` clip scrubbed by the charge, aim lane `ThrowPreview` that grows and is cut short by walls), let go and the real weapon leaves his hand (the held model is hidden, a copy `ThrownWeapon` flies) along a straight line, sweeping enemies (once each, `heft` decides launch / stagger / how much speed it loses), smashing props (`Destructible.blast`) and stopping at the first wall or at the end of its range, where it embeds at a slant. The same key again recalls it: it is wrenched out, homes on his hand with a limited turn rate through whatever stands in the way, and a `wthrow_catch` puts it back in his hand. One state machine owns the weapon (`IN_HAND, CHARGING, RELEASING, FLYING_OUT, EMBEDDED, RIPPING, RETURNING, CATCHING`). While it is away `PlayerStats.weapon_locked` stops the weapon slot being changed, `SkillController.weapon_ready` refuses sword skills (basic, power, skewer, leap, earthshatter), death and `revive_at` put it back in his hand. All numbers: `WeaponThrowSkill.DEFAULTS` and each weapon's `throw` profile (`tools/write_item_defs.py`). Only the first hit of a pass is a non-`secondary` hit (so on-hit affixes fire once). Test: `--only=weaponthrow`; look: `-- --skillshot=throw [--equip=Greatsword]`. Icon: `tools/blender/make_throw_icon.py`; clips: `tools/author_attack_anims.gd`. |
| `game/hotbar_layout.gd` | Which skill is on which key. The seven assignable skills fill the six numbered slots and the right mouse button exactly, so a change is a swap (`HotbarLayout.assign`). The live layout is `GameSettings.hotbar_slots` / `hotbar_alt` (saved with the settings, shared with `SkillController.hotbar`); the HUD's click-a-slot picker (`Hud.open_picker`) and Settings > Controls dropdowns both change it. Test: `--only=hotbar`. |
| `game/gamepad.gd`, `game/input_setup.gd` | Controller support: sticks, aim, labels, rumble; all buttons are ordinary input actions, rebindable in Settings > Controls (keyboard/mouse and controller columns). |
| `game/loading_screen.gd` | Loading bar with real progress (assets load on background threads) and rotating messages; the menu's Play and the pause menu's Restart go through it. |
| `game/dev/dev_harness.gd` | Developer tooling: the headless self-test and the screenshot/pose tools. Not part of the game. |
| `game/night_sky.gd`, `game/night_sky.gdshader` | The night sky behind every scene (stars, a moon, drifting cloud streaks). `NightSky.apply(environment)`; it is painted on every direction, below the horizon too, because the 3/4 camera looks down and only sees it beyond the edge of the map. |
| `game/item_effects.gd`, `game/ember_trail.gd` | What worn affixes do (data in `data/affixes`, written by `tools/write_affix_defs.py`; the hooks here are keyed by affix id). Several are built to combine with skills and with each other: fire states (Kindling, Pyrebound, Smouldering, Ember-Treaded), thrown and stunned states (Juggler's, Breaker's, Maelstrom), skill chaining (Virtuoso's, Vaultborn, Wildfire, Ramming, Impaler's, Quakebound). |
| Retired spinning attack | Cleave and Whirling are outside the playable kit. Maelstrom now stuns combo finisher hits, including Greatsword slam victims. The old spinning slash and inward pull visuals remain in `game/skill_fx.gd` for a future weapon with a circular attack. |
| `game/ui_theme.gd` | The one UI look (worn metal, bronze trim, ember accent, muted bars): the Theme for menus plus drawing helpers for the HUD (`UiTheme.draw_panel`, `draw_bar`, `text` with a shadow). Use these for any new UI so it matches. Status readouts are an icon and a number, no label (`UiTheme.draw_stat`; the icons `assets/icons/ui/*.png` are rendered in Blender by `tools/blender/make_ui_icons.py`, the potion is the hotbar's own). |
| `game/destructible.gd`, `game/debris_chunk.gd` | `Destructible`: breakable props (barrels, carts, gravestones, braziers; see `STATS`). The arena builds them in `_place`. `Destructible.blast(tree, centre, radius, damage, dir, force)` is how anything breaks them: swings, Power Strike, the Greatsword slam, fireballs, bloater bursts, Earthshatter, and (force 2+, damage 999) a Skewer charge or a Leap, which throw the pieces along the path. Clicking a prop sends the hero to swing at it. Breaking one frees its collision and re-bakes the navmesh, and may drop a potion, a healing orb or an item. |
| `game/loot_drop.gd` | `LootDrop`: an item lying on the ground after a monster died (tossed from the body, rarity glow and name, taken by clicking it or its name, or with the use key when in reach: nothing is picked up by walking over it; its name is a plate the HUD lays out so plates never overlap, `Hud._layout_loot_names`). Monsters roll drops from `EnemyDef.drop_chance`/`drop_luck` (`Items.roll_drop`); `PlayerStats.pickup` fills an empty slot and sends anything else to the bag (rarity never swaps gear on its own), which goes to the town stash when a job run ends. There is no reward screen between waves any more. |
| `game/town/` | The whole connected world: the plaza and, beyond its north gate, the Crypt Road, all loaded together when Play is pressed (monsters included), with no loading when you walk out. `TownScene` builds both on one shared navigation mesh (`Arena.origin_offset`, `Arena.shared_region`, `Arena.open_south`), owns the hero, camera, HUDs and a `RunDirector` in `world_mode` (gold per kill, no waves; a fallen hero is dragged home with `Player.revive_at`), blends the light colder and foggier the further out the hero is, charges the clan's food once per expedition and, on coming back inside, stashes the bag and records the gear. Quests are standing and need no accepting: `TownState.board` holds what is posted, `report_progress` marks a met objective `ready`, Warden Hale gets a coin over his head (`TownNpc.set_reward_marker`) and `claim_ready` pays it, posts the next quest and `CryptRoad.regrow()` corrupts the road again. Developer flags: `-- --timing --ready --townshot --at=x,z`. Test: `--only=world`. The old hub description follows. `TownScene` (the plaza, physical gate, stone, board, stash, people), `TownNpc`, `TownPanel` (trader, jobs, healer, recruit, stash), `TownHud`, `TownStage` (acts the sim out: people walk to each other to gossip, argue and brawl, and to the barrels, the stone and their stations to drink, pray and work), `TownSim` (what NPCs do all day: activities weighted by traits, likes and dislikes, opinions, brawls, leavers, events), `TownState` (everything that outlives a run: gold, food, gear, stash, moods, opinions, the board; saved to `user://town.json`). Play on the main menu opens the town; walking through its gate starts the chosen job, and without a job the hero stays in town. A finished or failed job comes back here. Design notes: [docs/zombasite-world-and-npcs.md](docs/zombasite-world-and-npcs.md). |
| `game/town/quests.gd`, `game/data/quest_def.gd`, `data/quests/` | Quests and events, Zombasite-style (docs/zombasite-world-and-npcs.md section 5). A `QuestDef` has a chance to turn up, a kind (`kill_group`, `slay_unique`, `fetch`, `matter` (a decision in town), `world` (the road changes for a few days)), rewards and penalties that move gold, food, potions, items and moods, a deadline in days (a day passes each time the hero comes back from the road), and `links` that start other quests when it starts, is done or fails. `Quests` is the pure rule set over `TownState.quests` (post, tick the clocks (a quest every 180-300 s, a matter every 120-195 s), measure kills, claim, decide a matter, expire, chain). The world calls in: `RunDirector` reports kills and drops, `CryptRoad.spawn_unique` puts a named monster on the road, `QuestPickup` is a quest item to walk over, `TownScene` posts, shows and re-populates, and a coin floats over whoever has something for the hero. Written by `tools/write_quest_defs.py` (15 quests). Checks: `--only=questlogic`, `--only=questworld`; developer view: `-- --townshot --quests`. |
| `game/town/monsters.gd`, `chronicle.gd`, `monster_mark.gd`, `plot_sign.gd` | What the monsters do on their own (Zombasite's named monsters, nemeses and plots). `Monsters` is the pure rule set over `TownState.monsters`: a named monster rises on the road (at most two), grows 0.3 levels every day, and once grown starts a plot (`raid`: hits the gate for food/gold/nerve; `altar`: a standing world effect until its maker dies; `uprising`: a horde plus a count bonus; `scout`: robs and roughs up someone) that counts down in days. The hero's killer becomes a nemesis (`hero_fell`; an unnamed killer is named 60% of the time, `TownScene._killer_takes_credit`, via `Actor.last_hit_by`); killing a named monster pays a bounty, drops items and ends its plot (`RunDirector._report_to_quests`). Shown to the player by: `Chronicle` (every event with its day, `TownState.chronicle`, the panel opens with J / D-pad left / Hale's board), cards with a bar per plot on the left of the HUD (`Monsters.cards`), edge-of-screen arrows (`TownScene._pointers`), minimap diamonds/triangles, `MonsterMark` (diamond, name, level, nemesis glow, growth) and `PlotSign` (altar, war banner and smoke, bones) on the road, a taunt line when the hero comes near, and gossip townspeople say (`Monsters.gossip`). `TownScene.populate_monsters` puts it all on the road. Each time the road is corrupted afresh `TownState.road_seed` changes, which restocks the gaps differently and rolls the road's **conditions** (`TownState.roll_road_conditions`: half the time none, else one or two compatible run modifiers: Fog, Darkness, Swarming, Spitters' Nest, Bloated, Shambling, Plague Road and so on). | `game/world/world_threat.gd`, `Items.tier_for_source` | **Five different things, kept apart. Hero Level does not automatically scale the world (a permanent project rule).** *Hero Level* (`TownState.hero_level`): how developed the hero is; scales nothing in the world. *World threat*: how dangerous a place or monster is, one float scale where an enemy spawned at threat T has `level_scale = T` (health x T, damage x (1 + 0.4 (T - 1))). Ordinary monsters take their stretch's `ZoneDef.base_threat` (`WorldThreat.for_distance`, `CryptRoad.threat_at`; today 1.25 Road In, 1.3 Graveyard, 1.4 Wood, 1.5 Crypt Approach); a quest's unique monster has its own absolute `QuestDef.unique_level` on the same scale (1.5-1.7 today). *Named Monster Level* (`mon["level"]`, +0.3 a day, +0.5 for killing the hero): it is spawned with that level as its own threat (`CryptRoad.spawn_named`, `Enemy.set_threat` as it grows), whatever stretch it lives in; `MonsterMark` is only the picture and touches no combat stat, so a level 5 nemesis can sit on the Road In. *Item tier*: the numbers on a drop, from its SOURCE only: `Items.tier_for_source(threat)` = 1 + floor(threat) (so 1.25 is tier 2, a level 5 named monster tier 6), coarse on purpose; ordinary kills use the dead enemy's own `level_scale`, named kills their level, props the stretch they stand in (`RunDirector.drop_tier`, `drop_tier_at`). *Rarity*: how special (`EnemyDef.drop_luck`, the roll chances), never the tier. Merchants and quest item rewards use explicit placeholders (`WorldThreat.MERCHANT_THREAT`, `MERCHANT_RARE_CHANCE`, `QUEST_REWARD_THREAT`, or a bundle's own `threat`) until they get a progression of their own. Test: `--only=worldprogression`. |
| XP flow (`HeroProgression` rewards, `TownState.add_hero_xp`, `RunDirector._pay_kill_xp`) | Where hero XP comes from. **Source -> rule -> `TownState.add_hero_xp(n, save_now)` -> `hero_xp` (the only store) -> `HeroProgression` derives the level -> `TownState.events` -> HUD plate and the open character screen.** Rules and numbers all live in `hero_progression.gd`: a kill is `EnemyDef.xp_reward` x the dead enemy's `level_scale` (min 1; zombie 1, ghoul 2, spitter 3, bloater 4, priest 5, brute 12); a named monster adds `40 + 20 x level + 25 per time it killed the hero + 25 if it had a plot`; a nest 18 when it breaks; the standing quest 120 on hand-in (`offer["xp"]`); a dynamic quest the `"xp"` in its own reward (`QuestDef.reward`). ## Data first

Skills, enemies and affixes are Resources, not dictionaries in scripts.

* **Edit a number**: change the table in `tools/write_enemy_defs.py` / `write_skill_defs.py` / `write_affix_defs.py`, run it, and the `.tres` files regenerate. (The `.tres` files can also be edited by hand or in the Godot inspector.)
* **Add an enemy**: add an entry to `write_enemy_defs.py` (stats, model path, `behavior`, `params`, spawn rules). If it needs new behaviour, write an `EnemyBehavior` subclass and add it to `EnemyBehaviors.create`. Waves pick it up from its spawn fields (`min_wave`, `base_count`, `per_wave`, `max_per_wave`, `spawn_mode`).
* **Add a skill**: add a `SkillDef` entry. Skills of an existing `kind` need no code; a new kind gets a branch in `SkillController` (or its own state machine in `game/skills/`, like Skewer and Leap).
* **Add an affix**: add the catalogue entry, then the behaviour hook in `item_effects.gd` keyed by its id.

* **Town data**: `tools/write_town_defs.py` writes `data/npcs` (`NpcDef`: Marlow the trader, Hale the keeper, Maren the healer, Cutter the recruit, Dorn the smith), `data/traits` (`TraitDef`) and `data/modifiers` (`RunModifierDef`). A run modifier (it belongs to the run, not to a kind of objective) is only multipliers (enemy spawn weights, health, speed, size, ambient light, fog, reward) applied by `RunDirector`, so adding one is a table entry. A new NPC is an entry plus a Meshy model (`tools/assets.json`, then bake its animations).
* **Town buildings and clutter**: `tools/blender/make_town_props.py` builds the forge, inn, chapel, granary and the small clutter (anvil, crates, wood pile, trough, drying rack, laundry line) into `assets/models/town/<name>/model.glb`, using the item script's palette and weathering plus a densify/warp and plank, shingle and grime pass so they match the Meshy cottages. They are placed by name `town/<name>` in `TownScene._build_town`; `Arena._place` switches their vertex colours on.
* **Town layout**: the static town is the scene `game/town/town_layout.tscn`, edited in Godot. `TownProp` nodes under `Props` (model, height, collides, lit; a preview shows in the editor) are the buildings, walls, lamps and clutter; `Marker3D`s under `Spots` mark the stone, board and stash, while `Stations` marks where people go (`drink`, `pray`, `work_<role>`). The gate is a physical trigger, not an interaction spot. `TownScene` places each prop through `Arena.place_prop` and deletes the markers; scripts keep the NPCs, interactions, simulation, state and transitions. NPC homes are still `NpcDef` data. To add a building, add a `TownProp` and set its `model_name`.
* **Weapon styles**: a weapon's moveset is `ItemDef.profile` (written by `PROFILES` in `tools/write_item_defs.py`), read through `PlayerStats.weapon_profile` (swing speed is the weapon's own `speed` stat, so the profile has no second speed knob): `recovery` (how fast the blade comes back after each blow), `weight` (stagger and knockback of the combo), `arc` and `arc_damage` (a basic swing also hits the other enemies inside that many degrees, for that share of the damage), `finisher_time`, `finisher` (`slam`: the combo's last blow shakes the ground in front) and `style` (the tooltip line). The falchion is quick and light, the longsword (an empty profile) is the baseline, the greatsword wide, heavy and slow; `SkillController._weapon_style_hits` and the three hooks in `start_skill`, `tick_busy` and `_apply_skill` are all that read them. Check with `--only=weaponstyle`.
* **Items**: base items are data. `tools/write_item_defs.py` writes `data/items/*.tres` (`ItemDef`: id, name, slot, model, icon, base stats and how they grow per tier), read through `ItemDb`. An item in the world is still a Dictionary built by `Items.build` (`{def, tier, slot, rarity, name, base, stats, affix, flavor}`), but a save keeps only `{def, tier, rarity, affix}` (plus a unique's name) via `Items.to_save` and rebuilds it with `Items.from_save`, so changing a base's numbers or model reaches every copy and an item or affix that was removed is dropped on load instead of breaking the save. Save version 2 rewrote old whole-item saves (the version 1 migration step).
* **Road density**: `CryptRoad` puts every zombie and ghoul of a hand-placed group out `PACK_COPIES` (3) strong, then `_fill_gaps` walks a grid over the whole strip and drops a small group (kinds by stretch of road) wherever no group's home is within `FILL_COVER`, from the same seed every time; about 210 monsters in all, none within 22 m of the start. `RunDirector` puts unaware monsters more than 85 m from the hero to sleep (`process_mode` disabled) and wakes them inside 70 m, so the crowd costs little.
* **Skewer preview**: Skewer is an aimed skill (hold its key, let go to charge; right-click or dodge cancels). While held, `SkillController._update_lane` has `SkewerPreview` (`game/skills/skewer_preview.gd`) draw the whole lane the charge will run (blade-catch width, range plus skid, cut short at the first wall or prop by three rays) and the fan the three kicked enemies are thrown across, and lights the first three enemies the blade would take.
* **Jobs**: a job is `{name, location, objective, modifiers, reward}`. `JobObjective` (`game/run/job_objective.gd`) is the only place that knows what an objective means (`clear_waves`, the test arena's wave loop, and `destroy_nest`, the Crypt Road's three nests, which has no waves: `uses_waves`, `ends_run_when_complete`; kill a target, rescue, hold a place and so on get a case there). The board, town HUD and `RunDirector` ask it to describe, count and finish a job, so none of them mention waves. Modifiers start at an objective *stage* (`min_stage`), which for `clear_waves` is the wave number.

## Tests and CI

`godot --headless --path . res://game/main.tscn -- --selftest` runs every check in `game/dev/dev_harness.gd`; each check calls `expect(label, condition)` and the process exits non-zero if any fail. `--only=NAME` runs one (`gibs`, `swarm`, `enemies`, `leap`, `earthshatter`, `impact`, `fireblast`, `balance`, `items`, `autoattack`). `tools/run_godot.sh LOG SECS -- <args>` runs Godot and kills it the moment a script/parse error appears instead of waiting for a timeout. `-- --timing` prints how long each stage of starting a run takes. `.github/workflows/selftest.yml` runs the full suite on every push and pull request.

Screenshot modes (`--skillshot=…`, `--enemyshot`, `--enemyfight`, `--clipsheet=…`, `--poses`, …) write PNGs to `%TEMP%` for judging visuals by eye.

## Assets

Model textures are imported GPU-compressed (VRAM) and capped at 1024 px (hero 2048): lossless 2k textures made every prop take ~280 ms to load. Large binaries (`*.glb`, `*.res`, `*.jpg`, audio, video) are stored with Git LFS (see `.gitattributes`). Dropped-item models are made in Blender (`tools/blender/make_items.py`, painted on the vertices, no textures); the Crypt Road's props (`nest`, `shrine`, `crypt_gate`, `cottage_ruin`, `iron_fence`, `corpse`, `road_gate`) come from `tools/blender/make_crypt_props.py` into `assets/models/crypt/` (place them as `crypt/<name>`). Blender 5.2's glTF exporter writes white vertex colours for every material but the first of a joined mesh, so that script exports one mesh object per material; on the Steam Deck Blender is a Flatpak (`~/.local/bin/blender`), so give it absolute paths. Models, rigs, animations and icons come from Meshy via `tools/meshy.py` / `meshy_icons.py`; `tools/bake_anims.gd` bakes animations into small `anims.res` files.
